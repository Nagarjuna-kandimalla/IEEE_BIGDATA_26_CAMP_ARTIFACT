\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Online workflow allocation.}
Witt et al.~\cite{witt2019feedback} connect execution measurements,
resource prediction, and batch scheduling through feedback.
Sizey~\cite{bader2024sizey} trains multiple predictors and selects models
online using completed-task observations. Ponder~\cite{lehmann2024ponder}
adapts its sizing rule to available history and the relationship between
input size and peak memory, combining regression with historical bounds
and fallback requests. These methods motivate a workflow-specific adaptive
comparator.
Ponder is related work here; no new Ponder comparison is reported.

\textbf{Distributional prediction.}
NGBoost~\cite{duan2020ngboost} predicts parameters of a conditional outcome
distribution, enabling uncertainty estimates beyond a point prediction.
CAMP instead estimates selected memory quantiles and applies empirical
residual calibration. Neither a distributional output nor a lower median
prediction error alone establishes a better allocation policy: the
reservation quantile and calibration determine the coverage--cost
tradeoff. A comparison using identical prelaunch information is therefore
needed before drawing conclusions about these alternatives.

\textbf{Relationship to CAMP.}
WORKS introduced consumption-aware prediction and selective
auditing~\cite{camp2026works}. This paper treats that architecture as
prior work and examines its operational consequences through retained
component comparisons, downstream feedback, audit-cost accounting, and
live deployment records. The planned unseen-input study addresses a
further question that the repeated-input deployment cannot resolve.
