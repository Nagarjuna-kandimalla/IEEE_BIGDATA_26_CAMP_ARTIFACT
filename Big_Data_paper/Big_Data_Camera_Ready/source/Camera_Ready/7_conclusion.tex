\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Consumption feedback must be assessed through both allocation coverage
and reservation cost. The retained component studies show that adding
measurement or prediction signals does not improve every metric, while
audit accounting limits when a fixed allocation saving covers measurement
cost. The new live Bowtie2 executions extend this evidence beyond repeated
inputs. A warm pass reduces aggregate requests by 75.34\%, but new-input
coverage subsequently falls to 94.15\%. Skipping that pass from the same
cold checkpoint increases requests by 7.62\% and coverage to 95.78\%,
exposing a tradeoff rather than a uniformly preferable training path.
All tasks complete under a separately recorded execution allowance;
nominal request sufficiency and end-to-end resource savings remain
unestablished. Repeated trials across input studies, matched control-node
resources, and a frozen-model arm are needed to isolate these effects
and determine how broadly they hold.
